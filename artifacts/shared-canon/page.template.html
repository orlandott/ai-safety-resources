The Shared Canon

Nine AI-safety curricula were pulled apart reading by reading — 668 distinct works across the six with public reading lists. This page maps which papers the courses actually share, which are one syllabus's obsession, and how small the common canon really is.

What the overlaps say

There is no universal reading. Not one of the 668 works appears on four syllabi, and only four make it onto three: Hugging Face's Illustrating RLHF explainer, Anthropic's Scaling Monosemanticity, BlueDot's own Introduction to Mechanistic Interpretability, and Apollo Research's We need a Science of Evals. If the field has a canon, it is mechanistic interpretability plus RLHF — and it is thin.

The overlaps split into two separate canons. ARENA and Intro to ML Safety share the ML-engineering backbone (ResNet, AdamW, induction heads, representation engineering, the GCG jailbreak paper). AGI Safety Fundamentals and Technical AI Safety — both BlueDot courses — share the alignment canon (Constitutional AI, debate, weak-to-strong generalization, deep RL from human preferences). Almost nothing crosses between the two clusters.

The two CAIS courses barely overlap with each other. Intro to ML Safety assigns ~76 papers; AI Safety, Ethics & Society assigns essentially none — it is the Hendrycks textbook plus lectures. Their one shared work is An Overview of Catastrophic AI Risks, which is simultaneously the textbook's first chapter and a paper on the ML Safety reading list.

Lens Academy confirms its different bet. Its flagship course assigns zero academic papers — it is a guided read of Yudkowsky & Soares' If Anyone Builds It, Everyone Dies. Where Lens touches the shared canon it is through essays (What failure looks like, Simulators, We need a Science of Evals), while its compute-verification course carries a 9-paper technical canon that no other course touches at all.
