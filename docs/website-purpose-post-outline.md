# Post Outline: The Purpose of AI Safety Resources

*A draft outline for a blog/about post explaining why this site exists, who it's for, and how it works.*

---

## Working titles

- "Why AI Safety Resources Exists"
- "A Front Door to AI Safety and Alignment"
- "From Curious to Deeply Engaged: A Curated Map of AI Safety"

## Target audience

- The newly curious — heard about AI risk and want a credible starting point.
- The self-directed learner — wants depth across formats, not a single reading list.
- The community contributor — wants to suggest resources and help keep the map current.

---

## 1. Hook / Opening
- Open with the problem: AI safety and alignment are sprawling, fast-moving, and scattered across papers, books, films, and podcasts.
- The pain point: newcomers don't know where to start; the engaged lose track of what's worth their time.
- One-line thesis: **AI Safety Resources is a community-maintained map of the best ways to explore — and enjoy — the questions of AI safety and alignment.**

## 2. What the site is
- A curated, browsable collection of resources, not a wall of links.
- Spans many formats so people can learn the way they prefer:
  - Books (fiction & non-fiction), academic papers, films, TV shows, documentaries, podcasts, YouTube, and websites.
- "For the curious and the deeply engaged" — designed to serve both ends of the spectrum.

## 3. Why it exists (the purpose)
- **Lower the barrier to entry.** A trustworthy front door for people new to the field.
- **Respect different learning styles.** A documentary, a novel, and a paper can each be the right on-ramp.
- **Curation over completeness.** Human judgment about what's worth engaging with, not an exhaustive index.
- **Make the subject approachable — even enjoyable.** Frame these as questions to explore, not just risks to fear.

## 4. How it's organized
- Category-based browsing (one crawlable page per category).
- A curated **"Start here"** set for people who want a guided first step.
- Topic tags and lightweight client-side search to find resources across categories.

## 5. How it stays reliable (trust & quality)
- A single hand-edited source of truth (`resources.js`) with a no-dependency build step.
- Schema validation: required fields, valid categories, sane metadata, no duplicate links.
- Automated link-checking and runtime guardrails to catch dead links and quietly hide broken entries.
- Everything reviewable in git/pull requests — transparent and auditable.

## 6. Community contribution
- Anyone can suggest a resource via the on-site submission form.
- Contributors comfortable with code can add entries directly and open a pull request.
- The project is community-maintained by design — the map improves as people contribute.

## 7. Who it's for (recap with intent)
- The curious newcomer who needs a credible place to begin.
- The deeply engaged who want breadth and a way to track quality resources.
- Educators, organizers, and writers looking for vetted material to share.

## 8. Call to action / Close
- Invite the reader to **browse a category**, start with the **"Start here"** picks, or **suggest a resource**.
- Reaffirm the mission: making AI safety and alignment explorable, enjoyable, and accessible to everyone.
- Link out: homepage, the submission form, and the GitHub repo for contributors.

---

## Notes for the writer
- Keep tone welcoming and non-alarmist — "questions to explore," not doom.
- Lead with reader benefit; treat the build pipeline and guardrails as *evidence of trustworthiness*, not the main subject.
- Concrete examples (a named film, book, or paper per format) will make sections 2–4 land better.
